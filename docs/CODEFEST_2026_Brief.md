## 2. Track 1: Healthtech

### Problem statement
Malaysia has experienced a significant shift in its public health landscape, with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and lifestyle-related health risks accounting for an overwhelming percentage of the nation's medical burden. By 2025, clinical tracking data highlighted that nearly 1 in 5 Malaysian adults live with diabetes, while cardiovascular conditions and hypertension cases continue to scale rapidly across both urban and rural demographics (Ministry of Health Malaysia, 2024). These rising numbers place immense pressure on public hospital infrastructure, resulting in crowded emergency rooms, delayed specialist consultations, and fragmented long-term patient recovery tracking.

Despite the widespread availability of general health and fitness applications, many Malaysians remain highly vulnerable to chronic health complications due to a lack of active personal health monitoring, low clinical health literacy, and the complexity of managing treatment plans independently. The rapid growth of digital prescriptions, home-use biometric medical devices, and health tracking software has expanded opportunities for proactive wellness, yet most existing digital tools operate in structural silos. Patients frequently struggle to translate daily wellness data into actionable habits, while healthcare tracking systems remain disconnected from a patient's everyday routine (World Health Organization, 2025).

Vulnerable groups, particularly older adults managing multiple prescriptions and busy working professionals facing severe stress, are disproportionately affected by this disconnected ecosystem. Data shows that unmanaged daily lifestyle factors directly contribute to thousands of preventable hospital readmissions each year, while early warning signs of acute clinical deterioration go unnoticed until a crisis occurs. These gaps emphasize an urgent need for intuitive, everyday digital health tools that cross the threshold from passive tracking to active, preventative care.

### Suggested innovation areas
- AI-powered triage and symptom checkers
- Digital wellness and habit formation platforms
- Interactive health literacy tools
- Biometric telemetry and home-monitoring assistants
- Maternal and child IoT monitoring assistants

### Requirements
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | A working prototype of the core health tech feature |
| **Stretch goal** | Real/simulated patient data API integration, offline functionality, or a clinician dashboard |
| **Technology areas** | AI/ML, Mobile App, NLP, IoT Software, Data Analytics |

## 3. Track 2: Education

### Problem statement
Modernizing educational frameworks remains a vital step for national growth, ensuring that all learners can successfully navigate an increasingly complex, digital-first economy. In Malaysia, however, a critical divide has emerged regarding how effectively students absorb digital information, maintain long-term motivation, and access personalized career pathways. The rapid transition toward hybrid learning environments has introduced significant friction points in student engagement and academic focus across both public and private institutional settings. Research indicates that standard, one-size-fits-all digital materials frequently contribute to declining retention rates, cognitive overload, and unequal learning outcomes nationwide (Ministry of Education Malaysia, 2024).

Developing effective educational technology under these conditions requires overcoming deeply rooted behavioral and structural challenges. A primary issue is moving past passive, flat content delivery, such as long video lectures or unengaging PDFs, and creating intelligent systems that can adapt to a student's individual learning speed, unique strengths, and cognitive gaps. Furthermore, learners are constantly forced to combat intense digital distractions, making self-regulated study incredibly difficult without continuous, live supervision from educators. Studies highlight that the absence of structured, interactive progression frameworks and intelligent behavioral reinforcement often leads to high course abandonment rates and diminished academic drive (Kementerian Pendidikan, 2025).

Vulnerable milestones in a student's life, particularly the transition from secondary school to tertiary education or direct entry into the workforce, are disproportionately impacted by this lack of engagement. Many learners struggle to translate their academic progress into concrete, future-proof job skills, while accessible, up-to-date professional guidance remains difficult to acquire without highly specialized mentoring networks. These gaps emphasize an urgent need for intuitive, everyday learning platforms that cross the threshold from passive content repositories to active, smart educational ecosystems (UNESCO, 2024).

### Suggested innovation areas
- Adaptive learning pacing engines
- AI-powered peer study companions
- Micro-learning optimization tools
- Smart career trajectory navigators
- Gamified behavioral engagement trackers

### Requirements
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | A functional prototype of the core learning or guidance feature |
| **Stretch goal** | Real-time teacher dashboard, gamification with badges/streaks, or alignment with MOE's DSKP curriculum |
| **Technology areas** | Web App, AI/ML, Gamification, NLP, PWA/Offline, Data Analytics, Unity |

## 4. Track 3: Civic Tech

### Problem statement
Modernizing public infrastructure remains a major step for national growth, ensuring that town systems work smoothly for everyone. Within Malaysia, many citizens still face daily difficulties when dealing with basic public systems, local community issues, and social welfare programs. This gap shows a clear need for better tools, as important community areas like public transport tracking, flood warnings, and small-scale farming support often lack simple, unified digital networks to help people in their day-to-day lives. Research highlights that fragmented rural public delivery mechanisms frequently lead to significant delays in local authority response times and public dissatisfaction (Ministry of Housing and Local Government, 2024).

To fix these gaps, there is a clear call to design and build simple digital tools that visibly improve how citizens interact with public services and their local neighbourhoods. Hackathon teams are tasked with creating software solutions that make public systems more useful, easier to reach, and highly efficient across Malaysia. These projects should focus on solving real, clear community needs, ranging from everyday transport and waste management to emergency disaster response and welfare support for low-income households or persons with disabilities. Studies indicate that decentralized digital municipal tools can accelerate disaster relief coordination and increase public service engagement among marginalized groups by up to 40% (Malaysian Administrative Modernisation and Management Planning Unit, 2025).

By turning these ideas into real tools, this track aims to build stronger trust between local communities and public systems while making daily life easier for all Malaysians. Using open public data, smart mapping, and simple mobile apps will help bridge the gap between everyday users and local authorities. Ultimately, these innovations will help build a more connected, helpful, and community-focused digital environment that actively protects and lifts every citizen.

### Suggested innovation areas
- Live transit trackers
- Smart waste managers
- Flash flood alert systems
- Agri-support ecosystems
- Accessible welfare wallets

### Requirements
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | A working prototype demonstrating the core civic feature |
| **Stretch goal** | Integration with real government open data (data.gov.my), live map overlay, or a multi-stakeholder dashboard |
| **Technology areas** | GIS/Mapping, Mobile App, AI/ML, IoT Software, Open Data APIs, Data Analytics |

## 5. Track 4: Cybersecurity

### Problem statement
Cybercrime cases in Malaysia have risen sharply, from 35,368 cases in 2024 to 47,854 cases by September 2025 (Ministry of Digital, 2025). In 2025, RM2.7 billion in losses came from scam-related cases, an increase of almost 80% from 2024, while 5,735 cyber-related cases were reported nationwide. Common threats include online scams, phishing, identity theft, misinformation, and deepfake fraud, with 1,812 phishing cases causing RM65.5 million in losses in 2024 (Gardaffy, 2025).

Although many cybersecurity solutions have been created, many Malaysians remain vulnerable due to limited cybersecurity awareness, technical knowledge, and the complexity or cost of existing tools (Kementerian Komunikasi, 2025). The growing use of digital banking, e-wallets, online shopping, and social media has further expanded opportunities for cybercriminals, while emerging threats such as deepfake scams have resulted in over 454 investigated cases and RM2.72 million in losses since early 2024 (FocusM, 2025).

Vulnerable groups, particularly older adults and individuals with low digital literacy, are especially affected. In 2024, 1,916 elderly victims aged 61 and above lost RM255.4 million to online scams, while 8,022 victims aged 50 and above lost a total of RM780 million (Bernama, 2024). These statistics highlight the urgent need for accessible cybersecurity education and protection.

Therefore, there is a growing need for cybersecurity solutions that help Malaysians identify, prevent, and respond to digital threats. CyberSecurity Malaysia's 2025 awareness study, involving 12,186 students and 1,260 parents, further reinforces the need for greater cybersecurity awareness and digital resilience (Ley, 2023). By enhancing digital trust, protecting personal information, and encouraging safer online behaviour, these innovations can contribute towards a more secure, inclusive, and resilient digital ecosystem for all Malaysians.

### Suggested innovation areas
- AI-powered scam and phishing detection
- Digital literacy platforms
- Cybersecurity apps for older adults and first-time internet users
- Deepfake detection and media verification
- Personal cybersecurity assistants with real-time alerts

### Requirements
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | A working website or app with the core safety feature, usable without technical knowledge |
| **Stretch goal** | Integration with MCMC's scam database, crowdsourced community reporting, or WhatsApp bot deployment |
| **Technology areas** | Cybersecurity, AI/ML, Browser Extension, Python, Threat Intelligence |

## 6. Track 5: Finance (Financial Inclusion)

### Problem statement
Financial inclusion remains a significant problem in Malaysia for B40 families, gig economy workers, the rural population, street vendors, and micro-SMEs. Although the use of digital financial products has grown quickly, reaching 63% penetration across the population in 2023, many people and SMEs still have trouble accessing financial products, managing their finances, and taking part in the digital economy (Farid, 2025).

Micro-SMEs frequently struggle to access financing from financial institutions because of strict requirements and time-consuming approval processes, and often have to turn to their own funds or other sources of finance (Funding Societies, 2023).

Beyond financial inclusion, digital literacy has also become crucial for effective engagement in the digital economy. The use of digital payment tools has risen in Malaysia, but some people and micro-entrepreneurs may still find it hard to carry out digital financial transactions and to identify financial risks online. This may limit the adoption of digital financial products.

Consequently, there is a need for innovative fintech solutions that increase financial literacy, make financial management easier, and give access to financial services to those who currently lack it. Such solutions can bridge the gap between old and new financial systems and build financial resilience and literacy (Alliance for Financial Inclusion, 2024; Funding Societies, 2023).

### Suggested innovation areas
- Alternative financing platforms for micro-SMEs and underserved communities
- Smart budgeting and personal finance apps
- AI-powered financial literacy and coaching
- Digital bookkeeping and expense tracking for micro-entrepreneurs
- Inclusive digital payment solutions for rural areas

### Requirements
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | A working prototype of an app with the core financial feature |
| **Stretch goal** | Integration with open banking APIs, real-time fraud scoring, or a multi-user view (trader, bank officer, etc.) |
| **Technology areas** | Fintech, Web/Mobile App, Data Analytics, AI/ML, Open Banking APIs, Blockchain (optional) |

## 7. Deliverables and judging

### Preliminary round
**Submit:**
- Source code (GitHub repository link)
- Slides (structure below)
- 3-minute demo video

### Grand finale
**Finalists prepare:**
- Project poster (A1)
- Presentation slides
- Functional prototype or live demo
- 5-minute live pitch
- Q&A with the judging panel

### Judging weights
| Criterion | Preliminary | Grand finale |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | 30% | 30% |
| **Technical Execution** | 30% | 25% |
| **Usability** | 25% | 25% |
| **Feasibility** | 15% | 20% |

### Required slide structure (preliminary)
| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Project & Team** | Project name, team name, member names and roles |
| **2. Problem & Track** | Track, problem statement, target users and why it matters, current limitations |
| **3. Solution & Value** | Solution description, unique value proposition, core features |
| **4. Technical Implementation** | Stack (frontend, backend, database, APIs), system architecture, integrations (APIs, libraries, datasets) |
| **5. UX & Design** | Step-by-step user journey; prototype with high-quality screenshots or wireframes |
| **6. Feasibility & Impact** | Real-world sustainability; scalability (more users/data); measurable social, financial or environmental outcomes |
